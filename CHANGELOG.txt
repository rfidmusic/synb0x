SYNB0X FIRMWARE CHANGELOG
=========================

Synb0x is an unofficial mod for the original 1010music Blackbox (not the Blackbox 2), applied as a
patch to the stock 3.1.9 firmware. It is a fork of blackbox-mod by Justin Joe (j3threejay), build
3.1.n. Newest versions first. Dates are commit dates (US Pacific time).

Every Synb0x build is made from the same stock file:
  Stock BLACKBOX.bin 3.1.9 SHA-256:
  281ae303d32e5eb52adca7817a648a34e4bd3848017f26673dd9df3905f1341d

Version names: 3.1.s, 3.1.t and S.1 are the releases. A third letter marks a test build of that line
(3.1.sr, 3.1.tr = Res remap; 3.1.ta / tb / tc / sc = AD envelope; 3.1.ua / ub / u0 / u1 = the "u"
line's filter in five versions: SEM, ladder (removed), stock 2-pole, stock 4-pole (removed),
Polivoks (up)).
Test builds were made for hardware testing and are not published.


== S.1 - release ==

Date:    2026-11-09
Status:  Published (GitHub main, tag S.1)
SHA-256: 5c2b24860fba306be715c03a7145c8d5b87f4d7500d3a32318cfad3bb5b037a0
Base:    3.1.v7 (test build: the 3.1.v line, after 3.1.u / 3.1.up)
Patch:   solo slice filter fenv filttab glide splash cpu od clock delay grain cache grainfast cave
Changes compared with the 3.1.t release:
- Filter Type per pad (Conf tab): SEM (12 dB/oct 2-pole SVF, as the Oberheim SEM) or Polivoks
  (12 dB/oct, slew-limited rail-clipping integrators, 2x oversampled); Morph LP -> notch -> HP on
  both. Res below noon runs from a flat Q 0.707 up to the stock noon value. The filter runs before
  the pad's Level, Pan and amp envelope on every pad type.
- Mod tab: Waveform / Trig, Rate / Beat, AD Atk / AD Decay, Atk Slope / Dec Slope. LFO Depth fixed
  at 100 % (the knob is gone; route amounts set the depth); LFO 1-Shot removed (the AD envelope does
  that job). Atk Slope / Dec Slope curve the AD stages without changing their times.
- Oscillator (Osc tab, Sample and Multisample pads): modulation source OSC for Filter and Level;
  Wave, Level, Octave, Tune / Fine, Pulse W, Osc and Osc Track buttons (Osc Track moved here from
  the Conf tab, now an on / off button). Pulse W, Level and Tune are modulation destinations.
- 480 MHz on revision V chips (479.23 MHz; stock clock on others); meter S.1 / S.1s.
- Long samples: an exact sample-cache index past 256 s.
- Granular: Fidelity picks the grain interpolation (HighQ cubic, else linear); grain window in ITCM;
  no full clear of the grain source buffer every block; the grain mix loop without a division per
  sample (the last two new in S.1, bit-identical to stock).
- Shared stereo readers, grain resampler and filter loops; copy loop by cache lines with preload;
  Saturation and delay-reader loops: all bit-identical where stock had them.
- Removed: Munchi delay, Room reverb, master compressor models (stock Delay, Reverb, Compressor).
- Preset format spec: docs/PRESET_FORMAT.md and docs/Blackbox_preset_format.csv.
Known: SEM self-oscillation builds slowly on low notes; AD starts late on granular pads.
Test builds 3.1.v1 .. 3.1.v7 (2026-10-15 .. 10-17) led here; they were not published.

== 3.1.up - Polivoks filter ==

Date:    2026-10-14
Status:  Test build, not published (branch polivoks-up, commit 2ca3f68)
Base:    3.1.u0 (3.1.ua's features; only the filter core differs)
Changes:
- The filter is modelled on the Formanta Polivoks (1982): a 2-pole (12 dB/oct) state-variable
  filter whose integrators are K140UD12 programmable op-amps used as slew generators, with no
  capacitors. Loud signals make the integrators ramp at a fixed rate instead of curving, and the
  outputs clip at the op-amp rails, which gives the aggressive, easily overloaded sound.
- The model runs twice per sample (as in Vult's oversampled filter examples, MIT) so the clipping
  aliases less; small signals stay in tune; cutoff up to 16 kHz.
- Self-oscillation from about 92 % Res is left free, as on the original: it grows to the rails,
  rough (not a sine), and sits about 5.6 semitones below the cutoff at every pitch, so it still
  follows KEY, transposed. Morph LP -> notch -> HP as before (the original has LP and BP).
- CPU: about 12,000 cycles more per voice than 3.1.ua in the stress test below (filter mean
  42,403 vs 30,367 cycles per stereo voice per block, with Fidelity, envelopes and Morph routes).
SHA-256: b099d20e495f3fcd57c157cbddcb0a01bb0dc0158357ec61be1f4cd95c4c048f


== CPU stress test (stress_cpu.py, emulator) ==

Every voice random (Fidelity incl. machine modes, Res up to self-oscillation, Morph, filter
envelope, AD, LFO Mode, pitch, Morph routes from every source except MIDI CC), every pad with
Saturation, master compressor + Room reverb + Munchi granular delay, voices added until one audio
block's budget is used. Real code for all of that, including the stock bus mix; the stock route
evaluation and voice bookkeeping are estimated (about 1,000 cycles per voice). The stock mixer,
sequencer and screen task are not included, so these are upper bounds: the on-screen CPU meter
shows the real load.

                                   3.1.tc    3.1.ua    3.1.up
  Voice, mean (cycles per block)   61,905    62,677    74,713
  Voice, worst                     99,333   101,912   110,745
  Master FX (comp + Room + Munchi) 501,439  501,439   501,439
  Voices until 100 %, 480 MHz          31        30        25
  Voices until 100 %, 400 MHz          23        23        20
  Voices at 80 % load, 480 MHz         23        23        19

The engine itself allows 16 voices per pad (Poly X) on 16 pads: 256 voice slots. CPU is the limit.

With a granular voice per pad (3.1.up), on the stock granular engine itself: a granular pad plays
through the sample voice FUN_08057fe0, whose 8 grain objects are each rendered by the stock grain
renderer at 0x08057db8 and respawned by FUN_08057ab0 (rand() for Scatter / Pan Rnd), run here in
the emulator (stock cubic resampling, the window table at 0x080d26ac, constant-power pan). Only
the sample-cache read is stood in for, by the stock float copy with each fetch's exact length.
Random grain size (30-200 ms), scatter, pan, pitch (rate 1.0 skips resampling) and Speed.

  Granular voice (cycles per block)  mean       worst      of which grains + fetch (mean)
  Grain Count 8                      175,972    403,566    108,089 + 19,693
  Grain Count 4                      124,675    219,002     65,395 + 11,090
  Grain Count 2                       91,268    133,785     37,018 +  6,060

One 8-grain granular voice averages 6.9 % of a 480 MHz core and can reach 16 % (short grains at
high pitch respawn several times per block). A granular voice (Grain Count 8) on all 16 pads
needs about 3.5 M cycles per block: over budget (2.56 M at 480 MHz) before any other voice plays.
What fits (granular pads + random voices on top, every pad with Saturation, master FX on):

                       480 MHz                     400 MHz
  Grain Count 8        10 + 2                      8 + 1
  Grain Count 4        15 + 0                      11 + 1
  Grain Count 2        16 (all) + 5                15 + 1

== 3.1.u1 - The stock filter as a 4-pole, self-oscillating - REMOVED ==

Date:    2026-10-13
Status:  Removed 2026-10-14: the same filter as 3.1.ua for fixed settings, and the SEM (3.1.ua)
         sounds better. The firmware file and the stock-u1 branch were deleted. History only.
Base:    3.1.u0 (3.1.ua's features)
Changes:
- Two of the stock filter's biquads in series (24 dB/oct): the first fixed, the second carrying
  Res. Res 0 is a flat 4-pole (no peak), noon the stock setting.
- Mathematically the same filter as 3.1.ua for any fixed setting: in the emulator their responses
  match to 0.1 dB, except the resonant peak at Res 80 % (+0.6 dB, because 3.1.u1 has no soft
  limiter inside the resonance). Differences are in how it behaves when driven: the resonance does
  not growl; drive is the input clip only (a saturator inside a biquad detunes it at low notes).
- Self-oscillates like 3.1.ua: within 0.54 cents, 0.5 level in every Morph position, strongest
  harmonic -88 dB.
- CPU: about the same as 3.1.ua at default Res, 13-21 % less with Res past noon.

== 3.1.ub - Self-oscillating 4-pole ladder (to compare with 3.1.ua) - REMOVED ==

Date:    2026-10-13
Status:  Removed 2026-10-14 for its CPU cost (about 21 % more than 3.1.ua at default Res): the
         firmware file and the ladder-ub branch were deleted. Kept here as history only.
Base:    3.1.ua (everything else the same; only the filter core differs)
Changes:
- The filter is a 4-pole ladder instead of the SEM-style pair of state-variable filters: four
  one-pole stages with resonance fed back around all four (the Moog idea), modelled without the
  usual one-sample delay so it stays in tune. Structure after the Faust libraries' moogLadder
  (MIT-style licence, credited in THIRD_PARTY.md).
- Res sets the feedback so each Res position gives the same resonant peak as 3.1.ua; from about
  94 % it self-oscillates into a clean sine at the cutoff, held steady by the same kind of level
  control as 3.1.ua (strongest harmonic -108 dB at full Res, -118 dB at 95 %; within 0.61 cents
  from 55 Hz to 10 kHz). KEY -> Filter at +100 % plays it in tune.
- LP keeps its bass as Res rises (passband compensated), so LP fully open still sounds like the dry
  sound and the click-free bypass works.
- Morph mixes the ladder's four stage outputs (the Oberheim Xpander way): LP 24 dB at 0 %, a deep
  notch at 50 %, HP 24 dB at 100 %, blended in between. Compared with 3.1.ua: the notch keeps the
  full level on both sides (3.1.ua's is about 12 dB down everywhere), in-between Morph settings let
  more highs through, HP resonates less than LP (as on a ladder), and the self-oscillating sine has
  the same level in every Morph position.
- Sound: softer knee at low Res, broader resonance, a slight bass bump around Res 50 % in LP.
- CPU (instructions per stereo voice per 256-frame block): 16,941 at default Res (3.1.ua 13,982,
  +21 %); 21,641 with Res past noon (3.1.ua 22,011, -2 %); 25,089 at full Res with Morph 50 %
  (3.1.ua 24,215). Stock 3.1.9: 6,325.
SHA-256: 09edd37f8127bed20f461b22a08ae21a40189d374dce3c8dc041ca0a57fa621c


== 3.1.ua - Self-oscillating SEM filter ==

Date:    2026-10-13
Status:  Test build, not published (branch ladder-u, commit 037c97e)
Base:    3.1.tc (so it also has the Res remap, the AD envelope and the 1-Shot button)
Changes:
- The filter can now self-oscillate. It is still the same 4-pole SEM-style filter with Morph
  (LP -> notch -> HP); only the top of the Res knob changes.
- From about 85 % Res the filter starts to ring, and from about 93 % up it sings on its own: a clean
  sine at the cutoff frequency, held at a steady level (about half of full scale) by a built-in
  level control, so it does not run away or blow up.
- It works in every Morph position: between LP and HP the notch no longer cancels the sine.
- The sine tracks the Filter knob: in the emulator it was within 0.61 cents of the cutoff from 55 Hz
  to 10 kHz. With KEY -> Filter at +100 % it plays in tune, one octave per octave, so the filter can
  be played as an oscillator from the keys.
- High Res drive is softened as oscillation takes over, so the sine stays pure (strongest harmonic
  -108 dB at full Res, -74 dB at 95 %).
- No clicks when sweeping Morph or pulling Res back down (emulator checks).
- CPU: about the same as 3.1.tc (13,982 vs 13,751 instructions per stereo voice per block at
  default Res).
- Known limits (from development notes): low notes take up to about 0.4 s to build up from silence
  (faster with input signal); the oscillation stops when the voice is flagged silent, for example
  when a one-shot sample has ended.

== 3.1.t - Filter Morph (current release) ==

Date:    2026-10-11 (first published and updated in place several times the same day)
Status:  Release, updated in place (tag 3.1.t, branch morph, commit c67a2d6)
Base:    3.1.s (all of its features, with Type replaced by Morph)
Changes in the first 3.1.t release:
- Filter Morph replaces the Type control. On the Filt tab, the Morph knob moves the 24 dB/oct
  filter continuously from LP (0 %) through a notch (50 %) to HP (100 %). Band-pass is no longer
  available.
- Morph is a modulation destination: LFO, VEL, KEY, FENV and the MIDI sources (pitch bend, mod
  wheel, volume, pan) move it per voice.
- Older presets: a pad saved on 3.1.s / 3.1.9-syn with Type HP becomes Morph 100 %; Type BP becomes
  Morph 50 % (the notch), so re-set those pads. This happens the first time the pad plays; save the
  preset to keep it.
Updates published in place the same day:
- Click fixes: turning Filter to fully open and back mid-note clicked loudly, and moving Morph (by
  hand or by LFO) made small zipper clicks. The filter now switches off only once its cutoff has
  glided all the way, fades back in when it restarts, and ramps the Morph mix smoothly.
- Less CPU: the soft clipping in the filter's Res drive and in Saturation was made cheaper (filter
  at high Res about 21 % less, Saturation about 14 % less), same sound.
- Morph reworked as a plain crossfade, like the Oberheim SEM's mode knob: the first version mixed
  LP and HP at full level, so most of the knob sounded nearly unfiltered apart from a narrow notch.
  Now the sound tilts steadily across the whole knob and is about 12 dB quieter at the notch. Chosen
  on hardware over an equal-power version (test build 3.1.t1).
- The CPU meter now actually shows on the device, next to the version ("3.1.t avg/peak%").
- New companion apps (no firmware inside): Synb0x Firmware Patcher (Windows / macOS; patch a stock
  3.1.9 file to 3.1.t or 3.1.s) and Synb0x Preset Converter (rewrites pre-Synb0x presets so filtered
  pads sound as they did; also convert_presets.py on the command line).
